**Situation:** a sourcing pipeline struggled to find a usable public reference photo for a specific
item. The owner supplied their own photo of the exact physical item instead. That photo was copied
directly into the run's working folder and used as the item's reference, with no further re-derivation
or second-guessing.

**Lesson:** when the owner supplies their OWN photo of the exact item a sourcing step is looking for,
that photo is an authoritative source, not just one more candidate to validate the way a scraped/
AI-generated candidate would be. The owner has ground truth a web search or a generative tool cannot
match (they own the physical item) — treating it with the same skepticism as an untrusted source wastes
effort and risks discarding the best available reference.

**Apply next time:** when a sourcing/review step receives a reference photo the owner explicitly took or
supplied for the specific item, accept and use it directly (copy it into the run's own asset folder) —
don't re-run the sourcing search as if the need were unmet, and don't apply the same quality bar
(resolution, framing, background) you'd apply to an unverified third-party source, since the point is
provenance, not surface quality.
