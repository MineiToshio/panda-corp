**Situation:** a proposed monetization path for a franchise-companion app assumed "free + donations"
avoids IP conflict (no direct sale of the IP holder's content). The IP holder's own PUBLISHED fan-content
policy directly addresses adjacent cases (crowdfunding fan projects, commercial use of AI-assisted fan
art) with explicit restrictions, and treats analysis/commentary/criticism as its own, narrower, case-by-
case legal question — not a blanket safe harbor the way "transformative commentary" is sometimes assumed
to be.

**Lesson:** "free, donation-funded, and clearly fan-made" is not a generic legal safe harbor for building
on someone else's IP — a real, named rights-holder policy can explicitly restrict adjacent monetization
mechanisms (crowdfunding, AI-assisted commercial fan art) that a generic assumption would have missed,
and whether "analysis/commentary" protects a SPECIFIC product still needs its own case-by-case check, not
an inherited assumption from a different fan-content category.

**Apply next time:** before proposing or assuming any monetization model for a product built on a named
copyrighted IP/franchise, find and read that IP holder's actual published fan-content/licensing policy
(most active franchises publish one) rather than reasoning from general "fair use"/"fan project" intuition
— name the specific restriction or ambiguity found, and treat "is our specific use (analysis, companion,
tracker) covered" as requiring its own verification, not an inherited blanket answer.
