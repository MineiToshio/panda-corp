**Situation:** a web-research sweep to find all brand collaborations for a collectible line ran
English-only queries and missed a real, documented collaboration (a regional retail tie-in sold through
Kinder) because that specific product line was marketed and discussed almost exclusively in
Spanish/Italian-language retail and fan sources, not English ones.

**Lesson:** "did a thorough web search" and "did a thorough web search in every language the subject
actually lives in" are different claims. Collaborations/products tied to a specific retailer or regional
market (e.g. a European confectionery brand, a regional convention exclusive, a country-specific
retailer) are often covered almost entirely in that market's own language — an English-only query set
can return confidently "complete" results while silently excluding an entire category of real items that
simply never surfaced in English-language sources. This is a blind spot that does not announce itself:
nothing in an English-only result set looks incomplete from the inside.

**Apply next time:** when researching a catalog/list/landscape that could include region-exclusive
items (a retailer tie-in, a country-specific release, a market-local collaboration), do not treat an
English-only sweep as complete. Identify which languages/regions are plausible homes for an exclusive
(based on the brand's known regional partners or markets) and run at least one targeted query per
plausible language/region before declaring the research finished — or explicitly flag the sweep as
English-only-and-partial if that check is skipped.
