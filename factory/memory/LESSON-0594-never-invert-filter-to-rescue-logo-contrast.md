**Situation:** a brand/logo mark had low contrast against its surrounding background; an `invert` filter
would fix the contrast mechanically but changes the mark's actual colors.

**Lesson:** the owner explicitly rejected `invert` as a fix for a low-contrast logo, even when the
inverted result has objectively better contrast — brand identity (the mark's own color) outweighs a
mechanical contrast fix that distorts it.

**Apply next time:** never reach for an `invert` filter to rescue a logo's contrast. Treat low logo
contrast as a problem to solve with layout/background choices (padding, a contrasting backdrop chip,
sizing) or by asking for an alternate official asset — never by filtering the mark's own colors, even when
the owner otherwise accepts a low-contrast result.
