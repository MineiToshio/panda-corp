**Situation:** a full career-copy/LinkedIn rewrite pass surfaced a cluster of recurring, owner-stated
content rules that a naive rewrite kept violating (over-claiming credit, wrong tense, literal
translation, incoherent bullets, ambiguous titles).

**Lesson:** career-copy has its own checklist distinct from portfolio/case-study copy (LESSON-0129,
LESSON-0221) and from general AI-tell style rules (LESSON-0206): (1) bilingual copy is localized, never
translated literally — EN has priority, ES carries the same idea in natural phrasing; (2) never claim
sole credit for team work — frame contributions as accountability, not ownership; (3) prefer plain,
widely-recognized English words over jargon a non-native reader may not know; (4) don't repeat shared
context (e.g. a company name) across adjacent entries that already establish it; (5) company descriptions
are present tense unless the company is known closed, in which case use a tense-neutral phrasing rather
than a false present; (6) every bullet must be internally coherent — describe one thing, never pair
unrelated facts, never name something the reader can't picture; (7) a de facto lead title reads as
"Formal title (Lead Engineer)", not "(Tech Lead)" unless Tech Lead was the literal official title —
the latter phrasing reads as two competing titles.

**Apply next time:** run any CV/LinkedIn copy draft through this 7-point checklist before finalizing;
localize bilingual copy rather than translating; verify tense/credit/coherence/title-phrasing explicitly,
not just prose quality.

**Calque-detection diagnostic (facet 1, sharpened):** when auditing an ES draft for literal translation,
flag any abstract or metaphorical NOUN PHRASE that could instead be a plain subject-plus-verb sentence —
this tell generalizes past career copy to any ES draft translated from an EN-drafted source: a literal
cognate ('caso de borde'), a vague metaphor ('se diluyó'), or a writerly abstraction ('lo que sobrevive...')
are three surface shapes of the same underlying problem, each fixed by asking "what would a plain
subject+verb sentence say here?" rather than polishing the noun phrase.
