**Situation:** across one case-study copy-revision pass, the owner gave a tight, consistent set of
corrections on three distinct copy surfaces: decision titles, the metrics/glance bar, and number
formatting conventions — each correction concrete and checkable, none a matter of taste alone.

**Lesson:** case-study copy has house rules at a level of specificity below what LESSON-0129's broader
"concrete over clever" taste captures — these are checkable style/content rules, not just tonal
preferences:
1. **Decision titles** must be self-explanatory to a cold reader who reads ONLY the title (what was done +
   why it mattered), must not admit any negative reading (a title that reads as "I noticed this too late"
   or "I never had a plan B" fails even if the body explains it well), and must stay short — roughly 8-9
   words / 60-70 characters in English; adding clarifying context to fix a vague title is a trap that can
   silently produce a rejected ~18-word title, so cut the second clause into the body instead of lengthening
   the title.
2. **The metrics/glance bar** holds IMPACT only — the owner's achievements or the product's value points —
   never descriptive trivia (a count of places something appears, a "1 of many" framing, a count of
   limitations). Tone throughout is formal and direct, never social-media clickbait phrasing.
3. **Number formatting:** the plus sign goes BEFORE the number (`+40`, not `40+`); a result line must never
   repeat a stat already shown elsewhere; consecutive decision titles must not start with the same verb.
4. **Numbers must be sourced, never guessed** — if the real figure isn't known, say so rather than
   estimating; a guessed percentage is worse than no number at all.

**Apply next time:** when drafting or reviewing case-study titles/metrics/numbers, run this four-point
checklist explicitly rather than relying on general copy taste: title passes the cold-reader-title-only
test and stays under ~9 words; metrics bar contains only impact, never trivia; numbers use the
plus-before convention and never repeat across the page; every number is sourced from the owner or a
verifiable record, never estimated.
