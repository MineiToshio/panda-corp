**Situation:** asked (implicitly, via a "feels corporate" critique) to make a design direction feel less
corporate, a reworked version switched to a thicker/rounder display typeface and added more structural
elements (a hero band, a featured strip, spanning tiles). The owner rejected it as disordered/chaotic and
preferred the original, sober direction over the "less corporate" rework.

**Lesson:** "less corporate" is commonly (mis)translated as "heavier/bolder/rounder type" and "more visual
elements" — but for at least this kind of feedback, what actually reads as corporate is
sterility/genericness, not restraint; and what reads as "too much" is clutter/density, not type weight.
Piling on new structural elements to chase a vibe can cost the clarity the owner liked in the first place,
independent of whether the vibe target was hit.

**Apply next time:** when a brief asks for a vaguer feel adjustment ("less corporate", "more fun", "more
premium"), isolate it to ONE lever at a time (e.g. palette/imagery OR type OR layout density — see
LESSON-0308) and test whether the EXISTING structure already reads as the target feel with that one
change, before assuming more/heavier/busier is required. Re-verify it didn't trade clarity for vibe.
