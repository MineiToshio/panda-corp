**Situation:** a plan to avoid licensing a copyrighted map asset proposed AI-transforming the original
protected artwork into a visually distinct image, reasoning that since the output looked different, it
would sidestep the rights holder's copyright. Legal analysis clarified this is incorrect, and the owner
decided map sourcing must instead be reviewed on a per-work basis.

**Lesson:** running a protected work through an AI transformation (image-to-image, style transfer, or
similar) to make the OUTPUT look visually different does not remove its status as a derivative work of
the original protected EXPRESSION — copyright protects the specific creative expression (composition,
layout, exact visual choices), not just the literal pixels, and a transformation that is clearly derived
from that expression inherits the exposure regardless of how different the final image looks. The
underlying FACTS a protected work conveys (e.g. the geography/place-names a map depicts) are not
themselves protected — an independently-drawn map created from the textual facts in a book, a properly
licensed map, or explicit permission are the legitimate routes; "disguise the source via AI" is not.

**Apply next time:** when a plan proposes AI-transforming a copyrighted asset specifically to avoid
licensing it, flag it as still a likely derivative work and route the decision to one of the legitimate
alternatives instead: licensed/permitted use of the original, or an independently-created asset built
from the underlying facts rather than the original's expression — decided per-work, not assumed safe as
a blanket policy.
