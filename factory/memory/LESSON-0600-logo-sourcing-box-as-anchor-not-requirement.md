**Situation:** funko-tracker needed a franchise/brand logo per catalog item, using the physical box as the
starting reference. An early rule required the logo to literally appear somewhere ON the box before
accepting it — this excluded trivial, easy-to-source cases (e.g. "Birdman") whose logo is well-known and
findable by a normal web search, just not printed on that particular box. Separately, some items (real
people, or franchises with no distinct brand mark of their own) have no logo to find at all; the box's own
category header text (e.g. "Director", "American History", "Tennis") was used as the identity graphic
instead.

**Lesson:** a physical package/box is an **identity anchor** (it confirms WHICH entity/franchise this item
belongs to) — it is not itself a **sourcing requirement** (the logo doesn't have to appear on it to be the
right logo). Conflating the two over-constrains sourcing and silently drops easy, unambiguous cases. When
no dedicated logo exists for an entity at all, its classification/category label (as printed on the
package) is a legitimate fallback identity graphic, not a placeholder to avoid.

**Apply next time:** for a catalog/collectible app sourcing a per-entity identity graphic from physical
packaging: (1) treat the box as confirming identity, then search normally (by name) for the entity's own
logo rather than requiring it be visible on that specific box; (2) when an entity genuinely has no logo of
its own, fall back to its printed category/classification header as the display identity rather than
leaving it blank or forcing an ill-fitting generic mark.
