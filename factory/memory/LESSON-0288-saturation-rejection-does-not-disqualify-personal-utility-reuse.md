**Situation:** LESSON-0156/0157 already warn against carding an idea just because it SOUNDS novel when
the market is already crowded/saturated with near-identical products — that verdict is about whether a
concept is a viable MARKET BET. Separately, the same underlying concept can resurface framed around a
concrete, evidenced PERSONAL workflow the owner actually has (DR-033's "return_type: personal" category,
`factory/ideas` schema) — a different axis entirely from market viability.

**Lesson:** a market-saturation discard answers "is this a good market bet", not "does the owner
personally need this enough to justify building it for themselves". When a previously-rejected concept
resurfaces attached to a concrete, evidenced personal workflow (not a re-assertion of generic market
demand), it is a GENUINELY NEW evaluation, not a repeat of the market verdict — re-evaluate it on its own
terms rather than auto-rejecting by citing the earlier market-saturation discard.

**Apply next time:** before re-discarding a concept that already carries a market-saturation verdict,
check whether the new framing is actually a personal-utility case (a concrete workflow the owner
describes having, independent of competitor count) — if so, evaluate it against personal-return
criteria, not the market-viability bar that killed the earlier framing, and don't duplicate the existing
idea card; update/reframe it instead.
