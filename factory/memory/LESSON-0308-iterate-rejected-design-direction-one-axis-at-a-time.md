**Situation:** reworking a design direction after owner rejection, a round that changed multiple
independent axes at once (real photography AND a heavier typeface AND new structural layout elements,
all in pursuit of a single vague feedback note) ended up losing specific elements the owner had
explicitly praised in the previous round, on top of not fully landing the original ask.

**Lesson:** bundling multiple independent changes into one iteration round makes it impossible to
attribute the owner's reaction to any single change, and risks silently discarding what already worked
while chasing what didn't. A round that instead changes exactly one axis (e.g. swap photography but keep
type/layout; or adjust one structural element but keep the rest) isolates cause and effect and preserves
prior wins by construction.

**Apply next time:** when reworking a rejected or partially-rejected design direction, pick the SINGLE
highest-signal axis the feedback points to and change only that one in the next round, explicitly
carrying forward everything else that was already working; resist the urge to "also fix" other things in
the same pass even when they seem related.
