**Situation:** early design-exploration mockups for a physical-collectible catalog/tracking app used
drawn silhouette placeholders for the product imagery. The owner found it hard to judge the visual
direction from silhouettes and asked for mockups built with real photos of the actual items instead.

**Lesson:** when a product's core content is photographic (collectibles, merchandise, inventory items,
anything whose visual identity IS the real object), a drawn or abstract placeholder is not a neutral
stand-in — it removes the exact signal (real texture, color, composition density) the owner needs to
judge layout, imagery-to-chrome ratio, and overall "does this feel right" fit. Placeholder silhouettes
make feedback rounds less useful, not just less finished-looking.

**Apply next time:** for any design-exploration pass on a product whose primary content is real-world
object photography, source a handful of real reference images (licensed/owned/public reference, clearly
marked and excluded from version control if unlicensed) before generating mockups, rather than defaulting
to drawn placeholders "for speed" — the time saved is lost again in a feedback round that can't actually
evaluate the direction.
