# Source audit for the software-factory testing decision

**30 claims: 17 CONFIRMED · 11 PARTLY · 1 WRONG · 1 NOT FOUND.**

**Evidence cutoff: 2026-11-21.** The strongest correction is about the intervention being measured. Asking a repair agent to create more test files, asking it to execute tests before implementing, giving it a specification, and giving it a skilled reviewer are four different experiments. The results below do not justify a blanket prohibition on implementer-written unit tests.

Each numbered row is one decision claim. Related measurements that support the same claim share a row. P refers to *ext-practices.md* and E to *ext-evidence.md*. CONFIRMED means the identified primary source reports the result for the stated population. PARTLY identifies a material qualification, transcription error or incomplete current roster. WRONG identifies a claim contradicted by primary evidence. NOT FOUND means I could not locate the claimed evidence after direct fetches and searches. Confirmation records what the authors report. It does not imply independent reproduction. Rates are percentages unless marked as percentage points (pp). Dates are publication or first-submission dates, with revisions distinguished. All prices below are US dollars.

## Evidence against a blanket testing policy

I searched specifically for test-first versus other prompting, same-model unit-test generation, no-test repair baselines, production deployment of generated unit tests, and negative results for separate verifiers. The following primary studies change how the two research files can be used.

| **TDD-Agent, 2026-08-17 (not pre-2026)** | On LiveCodeBench, ordinary chain-of-thought versus the same model’s test-first prompt gave pass@1 of **67.41%→70.04%** (GPT-5-mini), **67.46%→67.86%** (DeepSeek-V3.2), and **42.99%→44.87%** (Qwen3-Coder-30B). The test-first variant also beat the other reported reasoning-only prompts. **Decision implication.** The verified evidence supports independently specifying expected behavior on critical paths. It also supports keeping implementer-authored tests that reproduce a failure, distinguish a correct patch from a faulty one, or improve held-out performance. No study in this audit estimates the before/after escaped-defect rate from permanently removing such tests in an AI-only factory. The source record does not establish that such removal is safe, that a separate model always improves outcomes, or that two CI runs is an optimal limit for this factory. The outcomes that would settle those choices locally are regressions caught, defects escaping, and runtime or token cost on comparable changes.
