## Terms

- A **work order** (WO) is an approved piece of work with requirements, verification and dependencies. An **FRD** describes a feature. An **acceptance criterion** (AC) is an observable behavior the change must satisfy.
- A **coordinator** is the lead agent that holds the session’s authority, prepares the scope and environment, records evidence, and lands the result. A **builder** writes the implementation. A **reviewer** independently judges a pinned candidate.
- A **physical primary checkout** is the original Git directory where work usually happens. The **default branch** (typically `main`) is a ref that can be checked out elsewhere or nowhere. A **worktree** here means an additional checkout. A **lease** records exclusive authority with a token and generation, so old owners can be rejected.
- **G1** is today’s per-unit light gate. **G2** is the integrated feature’s selected test/browser gate. **G3** is the final queue proof. A **stamp** identifies a clean tree and the inputs under which it passed full verification. A **fail-first test** demonstrates the bug with an assertion on the faulty base in working setup. A command that fails during setup supplies no such proof.
- **Reviewed**, **certified**, **locally landed**, **push confirmed** and **deployment observed** are separate facts. A launched branch can be certified and held for the owner’s order.

## §9 · Red team of the four ideas

| Owner direction | Attack | Verdict and correction |
|---|---|---|
| Worktree only when needed | A clean Git status misses idle-but-write-capable sessions and stale children. A process can resume after expiry. The queue needs a checked-out main and launched pre-push will reject the unsupported shape. Open ports and caches can belong to other sessions. | **Adopt with enforceable ownership.** One managed writer under a common repository fence, a topic branch, a queue-owned ref advance after exact proof, and explicit checked-out default before push. Keep worktree mode wherever baseline ownership or supported recovery is uncertain. Lock scopes and pre-push identity matter as much as disk. |
| Parallelize in both procedures | Two builders with disjoint file lists can both need the same fixture, server, lockfile or generated state. Spawning stages also makes context, review, and landing slower on the critical path. | **Adopt conditionally.** Two code writers at most with separate writable checkouts, shape to real feature boundaries and actual host capacity, overlap independent evidence and review. Serialize coupled contracts, same-scope bug author/edit ordering, heavy shared fixture checks and landing. |
| One entry with two procedures | Hidden commands still create different invocation/runtime assumptions. A delta threshold can be gamed, and late risk discovery can launder a scope into a second apparently successful case. | **Adopt.** Two ordinary referenced files, one router and one shared proof contract. Pin original obligations and record the route and observed scope. Preserve the original case on a reroute. The conversation handles standalone design/prep as already defined. |
| Fix the full procedure, too | Keeping every engine step and replacing the wording retains the failure paths. Erasing the engine outright loses recovery, receipts and governance. A checklist alone is only an assertion. | **Adopt a trimmed attended build.** Reuse dependency, evidence and authority primitives. Keep one builder by feature and let a coordinator issue one commit per WO, using Git and evidence on recovery. Collapse repeated gates and prep, retain real cross-feature and independent proof. Keep the legacy route for manual fallback and broader unattended work until specifically qualified. |

**Risks accepted for the rollout.** Failures that a repetitive G1/G2 might catch early may be seen later at integrated review or queue. A short checklist gives less visualization than the old scheduler, and a coordinator becomes more important for state and recovery. Restrict safe concurrency to obvious independence and let deliberate holds use a worktree. Extra production-smoke assertions and independent review have real cost. Five outcomes can expose frequent friction. They give weak evidence about rare escapes or unrepresented scope. [44 §§12–15][44] · [45 red team §§4,6–8][R45]

**Limits beyond the redesign.** Ambiguous or incomplete requirements, fundamentally coupled changes, scarce quota, machine load, upstream outages, owner order and broken fixtures can still delay delivery. Reviewers can share a mistaken requirement. Tests still miss production-only data or configuration. A rollback cannot undo destructive data operations or spent money. Full queue time remains on the delivery path. The new procedures do not promise a first-review pass or a speedup. [44 §12][44] · [45 open questions][45]
